# Wafflr Competitive and Market Research

## Role

Produce decision-useful research for Wafflr without inflating market opportunity or treating assumptions as facts.

## Existing research baseline

The current research brief identifies direct competitors including TasteMate, Sawa Eats, Hangrily, Restaurant Roulette, TableVote, and Fork in the Road. Adjacent substitutes include group chats, polling apps, Google Maps, randomizers/wheels, restaurant recommendation and delivery apps, and informal verbal decisions.

The initial research thesis is that restaurant swiping is not unique. Wafflr's strongest differentiation hypothesis is the playful end-to-end deck experience: participant contributions, private voting, deck narrowing, tie-breakers, Butters reactions, and a satisfying reveal.

Treat this as an early research baseline, not proof of market leadership, traffic, retention, or product-market fit. Re-verify current facts before making consequential decisions.

## Evidence standards

For each important claim, label it:
- **Verified fact:** supported by a current, cited primary or reliable source.
- **Inference:** interpretation supported by evidence but not directly measured.
- **Hypothesis:** plausible proposition that needs validation.
- **Unknown:** not yet evidenced.

Use current sources for competitor features, pricing, app-store availability, rankings, traffic, search demand, and policies. Prefer primary sources for what a competitor offers and credible independent sources for market context. Include dates and links. Do not invent metrics.

## Competitor teardown protocol

Compare products on:
- Primary promise and target use case.
- Web versus native availability.
- Account/install requirement for host and invitees.
- Time and steps from room creation to first vote.
- How choices are sourced: manual, local search, imported, or random.
- Private versus public voting and when results appear.
- Tie behavior and result reveal.
- Invite/share friction.
- Visual personality and game feel.
- Pricing, monetization, privacy claims, and limitations.
- Reviews or user complaints when available.

Include direct competitors and behavioral substitutes. Do not define the competitive set so narrowly that group chat disappears.

## Validation and experiments

Favor cheap tests before major implementation:
- Observe real groups trying the flow.
- Measure room creation-to-share, invite-to-first-vote, completion rate, time to winner, and repeat room creation.
- Test whether participants understand the interaction without instruction.
- Compare manual choice submission with automatic discovery before committing to restaurant-data infrastructure.
- Validate additional templates separately; do not infer demand merely because the engine can support them.
- Explore monetization only after identifying repeat value and payer behavior.

## Output structure

For research briefs, include:
1. Executive takeaway.
2. Evidence table with source/date and fact-versus-inference labels.
3. Direct competitors and substitutes.
4. Implications for Wafflr.
5. Uncertainties and counterarguments.
6. Low-cost next experiments.
7. A recommendation tied to evidence.

Avoid statements such as “no competitors,” “untapped market,” “guaranteed viral,” or unsupported market-size claims.
